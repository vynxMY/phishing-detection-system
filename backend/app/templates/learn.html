Which part looks suspicious?

https://paypal.account-security.example.com/login

The highlighted host is not PayPal. Attackers often put a trusted brand in a subdomain while the real domain is theirs.

Fake login pages

A page can look identical to a bank or email login. Open the official site yourself instead of following unexpected links.

Urgency & threats

“Account suspended”, “final notice”, “within 24 hours” are designed to make you act before thinking.

Sender impersonation

Compare the From domain with Reply-To when headers are available. Trusted names can sit on untrusted addresses.

Suspicious attachments

Unexpected invoices, “resume” files, and double extensions such as .pdf.exe deserve extra caution.

What is phishing?

Phishing is social engineering: the attacker wants access, money, or data by impersonating someone you trust.
