Learn to spot phishing

Short lessons you can use even when PhishGuard is not open.

What is phishing?

Someone pretends to be a trusted person or company so you will click, log in, or send money.

Fake websites

A page can look identical to a bank or email login. The address bar is the part that matters.

Suspicious links

Hover before you click. Extra subdomains, misspellings, and IP addresses are common tricks.

Urgency & threats

“Account suspended”, “final notice”, “within 24 hours” are designed to skip your caution.

Fake login pages

Never enter a password from an unexpected email. Open the official site yourself.

Impersonation

Display names can be faked. Compare the From domain with Reply-To when you can see headers.

Suspicious attachments

Unexpected invoices, “resume” files, and double extensions such as .pdf.exe are high risk.

Which part looks suspicious?

https://paypal.account-security.example.com/login

The highlighted host is not PayPal. Attackers often put a trusted brand in a subdomain while the real domain is theirs.

PhishGuard’s explanations look for this kind of URL structure, plus urgent language and sender mismatch.
